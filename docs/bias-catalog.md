# Bias and framing catalog

116 directory concepts plus six media techniques. Definitions and examples below are original teaching material, not quotations from The Decision Lab. Every example is synthetic and has not been independently expert-adjudicated. A negative example excludes only its target concept, not every possible issue.

## 1. AI Literacy Gap

`ai_literacy_gap` · cognitive_concept

Comfort using AI exceeds understanding of its capabilities and limitations.

**Positive:** I use chatbots daily, so I am an AI expert; I did not know they can invent references.

**Target negative:** I use chatbots daily but still need training in how to evaluate their answers.

**Boundary:** Require evidence of a competence gap; frequent use alone is insufficient.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/ai-literacy-gap)

## 2. Accountability diffusion in AI

`accountability_diffusion_in_ai` · cognitive_concept

Responsibility becomes dispersed across AI stakeholders until nobody accepts ownership.

**Positive:** The editor blamed the model, the vendor blamed the editor, and the deployment team said checking factual errors belonged to neither of them.

**Target negative:** The editor reviews AI drafts and remains accountable for their accuracy.

**Boundary:** Distributed work alone is not diffusion; look for responsibility being disclaimed or left unowned.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/accountability-diffusion-in-ai)

## 3. Action Bias

`action_bias` · cognitive_concept

Action is preferred simply to act, without a supporting advantage over waiting.

**Positive:** Monitoring is more likely to solve this temporary glitch, but change every setting now so we are doing something.

**Target negative:** Restart the service because diagnostics show a restart will clear the fault.

**Boundary:** An evidence-based intervention is not action bias.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/action-bias)

## 4. Affect Heuristic

`affect_heuristic` · cognitive_concept

An emotional reaction substitutes for evaluation of an option's benefits or risks.

**Positive:** The logo makes me feel peaceful, so the product must be safe; I have not checked its specifications.

**Target negative:** I prefer this logo because I want a calming design.

**Boundary:** Emotion relevant to a subjective preference is not necessarily a distorted factual judgment.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/affect-heuristic)

## 5. Ambiguity Effect

`ambiguity_effect` · cognitive_concept

Unspecified probabilities make an option less attractive than one with known probabilities.

**Positive:** I prefer the urn with a known 50% winning chance to the one with an unspecified mix, solely because its odds are known.

**Target negative:** I prefer the first urn because a verified count gives it better odds.

**Boundary:** Ambiguity aversion can reflect a legitimate preference; do not equate it automatically with error.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/ambiguity-effect)

## 6. Anchoring Bias

`anchoring_bias` · cognitive_concept

An initial value pulls a later estimate toward it despite weak relevance.

**Positive:** After seeing the randomly assigned number 800, I estimated 750 attendees; without it I had estimated 300.

**Target negative:** I estimated 750 attendees from 750 confirmed registrations.

**Boundary:** Require an anchor affecting judgment, not merely the presence of a number.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/anchoring-bias)

## 7. Attentional Bias

`attentional_bias` · cognitive_concept

Attention selectively favors certain information while other relevant information is overlooked.

**Positive:** When anxious, I notice every hesitation in the feedback meeting but miss the equally clear statements of approval.

**Target negative:** I recorded both the criticisms and the praise before evaluating the feedback.

**Boundary:** Distinguish selective attention from a later judgment that weights negative evidence more heavily.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/attentional-bias)

## 8. Authority Bias

`authority_bias` · cognitive_concept

Status or authority is treated as sufficient grounds for accepting a claim.

**Positive:** The celebrity historian says this bridge design is safe, so engineering calculations are unnecessary.

**Target negative:** I accepted the structural engineer's conclusion after reviewing the supporting calculations.

**Boundary:** Appropriate reliance on relevant expertise is not enough to establish bias.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/authority-bias)

## 9. Automation Bias

`automation_bias` · cognitive_concept

Automated recommendations receive excessive trust despite reasons to check or reject them.

**Positive:** The spreadsheet total is visibly wrong, but the AI says it is correct, so I will approve it.

**Target negative:** The AI suggested a total; I recalculated it before approval.

**Boundary:** Using automation alone is insufficient; identify unjustified deference.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/automation-bias)

## 10. Availability Heuristic

`availability_heuristic` · cognitive_concept

Ease of recalling examples substitutes for evidence about frequency or probability.

**Positive:** I can instantly recall three elevator accidents from videos, so elevators must fail frequently, regardless of the maintenance statistics.

**Target negative:** The inspection database reports an increased failure rate, so I updated my estimate.

**Boundary:** Recency can make examples accessible, but the diagnostic mechanism is recall-based likelihood judgment.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/availability-heuristic)

## 11. Bandwagon Effect

`bandwagon_effect` · cognitive_concept

A belief or choice gains appeal because many others adopt it.

**Positive:** I thought the proposal was weak, but everyone now supports it, so I support it too; no new evidence appeared.

**Target negative:** I changed my mind after supporters supplied a successful pilot evaluation.

**Boundary:** Popularity must drive adoption; others can also provide useful evidence.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/bandwagon-effect)

## 12. Barnum Effect

`barnum_effect` · cognitive_concept

A vague description applicable to many people is accepted as uniquely personal.

**Positive:** This profile says I sometimes want company and sometimes solitude; it describes me so uniquely that it must know my personality.

**Target negative:** The profile contains broad statements that fit many people, including me.

**Boundary:** Agreement with a general statement alone is insufficient; require perceived personal specificity.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/barnum-effect)

## 13. Base Rate Fallacy

`base_rate_fallacy` · cognitive_concept

Relevant population prevalence is neglected in favor of case-specific information.

**Positive:** Only 1% of these messages are scams. The detector catches all scams but flags 10% of legitimate messages. A flagged message is therefore almost certainly a scam.

**Target negative:** A flag is worth investigating, but false alarms may outnumber real scams because scams are rare.

**Boundary:** Require an available relevant base rate being neglected; overlap with representativeness is possible.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/base-rate-fallacy)

## 14. Belief Perseverance (The Backfire Effect)

`belief_perseverance` · cognitive_concept

A belief persists after its supporting evidence is discredited; strengthening after correction is a distinct backfire pattern.

**Positive:** The photo was my only evidence. Even after its creator proved it was staged, I still believe it depicts the event.

**Target negative:** The photo was staged, so I withdraw the claim it supported.

**Boundary:** Preserved source umbrella: record perseverance and backfire as separate subtypes; persistence does not imply strengthening.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/belief-perseverance)

## 15. Benjamin Franklin effect

`benjamin_franklin_effect` · cognitive_concept

Doing someone a favor leads the helper to like that person more.

**Positive:** I disliked Omar, but after voluntarily helping him move, I found myself liking him more even though I learned nothing new about him.

**Target negative:** I liked Omar more after discovering that he had quietly helped my family.

**Boundary:** The direction is giving a favor then increased liking, not receiving kindness then liking its giver.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/benjamin-franklin-effect)

## 16. Bikeshedding

`bikeshedding` · cognitive_concept

Disproportionate discussion goes to easy trivial details while consequential issues receive little scrutiny.

**Positive:** We approved the unexamined infrastructure plan in five minutes, then spent two hours debating the slide colors.

**Target negative:** We spent two hours resolving a safety-critical control setting.

**Boundary:** Judge time against importance; a small-looking detail can have major consequences.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/bikeshedding)

## 17. Bottom-Dollar Effect

`bottom_dollar_effect` · cognitive_concept

Spending the last available funds reduces satisfaction with the purchased item.

**Positive:** The meal was identical, but buying it with my last discretionary money made it feel less satisfying than when I had money left.

**Target negative:** The meal disappointed me because it arrived cold.

**Boundary:** Distinguish diminished purchase satisfaction from rational concern about an insufficient budget.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/bottom-dollar-effect)

## 18. Bounded Rationality

`bounded_rationality` · cognitive_concept

Limited time, information, or processing capacity leads to a satisfactory rather than globally optimal choice.

**Positive:** I had ten minutes to find a room, so I booked the first one meeting my price and location requirements.

**Target negative:** I compared every available room against a fully specified criterion and selected the highest-scoring one.

**Boundary:** A decision framework, not inherently an error; label satisficing only when constraints and stopping rule are visible.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/bounded-rationality)

## 19. Bundling Bias

`bundling_bias` · cognitive_concept

Bundled pricing weakens attention to the cost of individual components and can reduce their use.

**Positive:** I would attend each separately paid workshop, but the same workshops in a package feel already absorbed into one price, so I skip several.

**Target negative:** I skipped the workshops because they were cancelled.

**Boundary:** Underuse must relate to bundling; a bundle purchase alone proves nothing.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/bundling-bias)

## 20. Bye-Now Effect

`bye_now_effect` · cognitive_concept

Exposure to the word bye is proposed to activate buy through their shared sound and influence spending.

**Positive:** In this hypothetical randomized study, shoppers shown 'bye' subsequently spent more than the matched neutral-word group, with all offers unchanged.

**Target negative:** The cashier said 'bye' after I completed my planned purchase.

**Boundary:** A narrow proposed priming effect; an isolated word or purchase cannot establish the mechanism.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/bye-now-effect)

## 21. Cashless Effect

`cashless_effect` · cognitive_concept

Cash-free payment increases willingness to spend relative to physical cash.

**Positive:** With the same budget and price, I reject the purchase when paying in notes but accept it by tapping my card because the payment feels less real.

**Target negative:** I use a card to purchase the same planned item at the same price.

**Boundary:** Require a change in spending or willingness, not just card usage.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/cashless-effect)

## 22. Category Size Bias

`category_size_bias` · cognitive_concept

A particular outcome is judged more likely because it belongs to a larger category, although individual outcome probabilities are equal.

**Positive:** A fair draw selects one of ten equally likely tokens. Eight are blue and two are red. I say the specific token Blue-1 is more likely than Red-1 solely because blue is the larger group.

**Target negative:** Blue-1 and Red-1 each have a 10% chance; the blue category as a whole has an 80% chance.

**Boundary:** Distinguish an individual outcome from its category; larger categories can genuinely have greater aggregate probability.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/category-size-bias)

## 23. Choice Overload

`choice_overload_bias` · cognitive_concept

A larger choice set impairs choosing or satisfaction through comparison difficulty.

**Positive:** With six suitable lamps I chose easily; with sixty similarly suitable lamps I became overwhelmed and abandoned the purchase.

**Target negative:** I rejected all sixty lamps because none fit the required socket.

**Boundary:** Many options alone are insufficient; identify overload and its consequence.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/choice-overload-bias)

## 24. Cognitive Dissonance

`cognitive_dissonance` · cognitive_concept

Inconsistent beliefs, values, or behavior produce discomfort and efforts to resolve the inconsistency.

**Positive:** I value equal treatment but favor my friends at work. That contradiction bothers me, so I tell myself fairness does not matter here.

**Target negative:** I noticed that I had two inconsistent estimates and checked which calculation was wrong.

**Boundary:** Dissonance is a psychological state; resolution through honest revision is not itself biased reasoning.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/cognitive-dissonance)

## 25. Commitment Bias

`commitment_bias` · cognitive_concept

A prior commitment is maintained or escalated despite evidence that it should be reconsidered.

**Positive:** I publicly backed this process, so we must keep it even though the trial shows it fails and switching has no additional cost.

**Target negative:** We will keep the process because the new trial demonstrates it works better.

**Boundary:** Public consistency can distinguish commitment bias from a specific appeal to unrecoverable expenditure.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/commitment-bias)

## 26. Confirmation Bias

`confirmation_bias` · cognitive_concept

Evidence is sought, interpreted, or accepted selectively to support an existing belief.

**Positive:** I believe remote work fails, so include the negative studies and omit the equally rigorous positive ones.

**Target negative:** Include studies both for and against remote work using the same quality criteria.

**Boundary:** Disagreement alone is not confirmation bias; identify asymmetric treatment of evidence.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/confirmation-bias)

## 27. Decision Fatigue

`decision_fatigue` · cognitive_concept

Repeated decision-making is followed by mental exhaustion and poorer or more avoidant choices.

**Positive:** After choosing among suppliers all day, I felt too drained to compare the final bids and accepted the default without reading them.

**Target negative:** I accepted the default after checking that it offered the best terms.

**Boundary:** Require the decision-load link; time of day alone cannot establish fatigue or its cause.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/decision-fatigue)

## 28. Declinism

`declinism` · cognitive_concept

The past is idealized while the present or future is judged to be deteriorating without adequate support.

**Positive:** Everything in society keeps getting worse; I dismiss the long-term improvements because the past must have been better.

**Target negative:** This specific service has deteriorated according to a consistent ten-year performance series.

**Boundary:** A supported decline in one measure is not generalized declinism.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/declinism)

## 29. Decoy Effect

`decoy_effect` · cognitive_concept

An asymmetrically dominated third option shifts preference toward the option that dominates it.

**Positive:** I preferred the €6 small plan to the €10 large plan. Adding a €10 medium plan with fewer benefits than the large made me choose the large, although the original plans stayed unchanged.

**Target negative:** I chose the large plan after its price fell below the small plan's price.

**Boundary:** Show the third option's dominance relation and a preference shift.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/decoy-effect)

## 30. Delegation Creep

`delegation_creep` · cognitive_concept

AI delegation expands gradually into new or consequential tasks without a corresponding review of suitability.

**Positive:** The tool first sorted notes, then drafted recommendations, and now decides which projects to cancel; nobody assessed its new role.

**Target negative:** Before expanding the tool's role, we validated the new use and assigned review responsibility.

**Boundary:** A temporal expansion is essential; one instance of automated advice is not creep.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/delegation-creep)

## 31. Disposition Effect

`disposition_effect` · cognitive_concept

Losing investments are retained and winning ones sold because of their gain-loss status rather than forward prospects.

**Positive:** I sell anything above my purchase price and keep anything below it until it breaks even, regardless of updated prospects.

**Target negative:** I sell the asset whose future outlook worsened, even though it has gained since purchase.

**Boundary:** Purchase price alone should not substitute for forward evaluation; taxes and strategy can justify trades.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/disposition-effect)

## 32. Distinction Bias

`distinction_bias` · cognitive_concept

Side-by-side comparison exaggerates differences that matter little during separate use.

**Positive:** Comparing the screens together, I predict a tiny color difference will dominate my enjoyment; using each alone, I cannot notice it.

**Target negative:** I compare two monitors because my work requires side-by-side color matching.

**Boundary:** Joint versus separate evaluation matters; a real use-relevant difference is not sufficient evidence of bias.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/distinction-bias)

## 33. Dunning–Kruger Effect

`dunning_kruger_effect` · cognitive_concept

Low demonstrated competence accompanies overestimation of competence and difficulty recognizing errors.

**Positive:** I scored 2 out of 20 on repeated calibration tasks but insist I am among the best and cannot identify my basic mistakes even when asked to review them.

**Target negative:** I am new to this subject and expect to make mistakes.

**Boundary:** Do not diagnose from confidence or disagreement alone; a single anecdote cannot establish a population-level effect.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/dunning-kruger-effect)

## 34. Einstellung Effect

`einstellung_effect` · cognitive_concept

A familiar solution blocks discovery of a better alternative.

**Positive:** I kept applying the old ten-step procedure and failed to notice the new two-step solution because I assumed the old method was the only route.

**Target negative:** I chose the familiar procedure after testing and ruling out the shorter route.

**Boundary:** Focus on fixation on a learned solution, rather than a general preference for a familiar tool.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/einstellung-effect)

## 35. Empathy Gap

`empathy_gap` · cognitive_concept

A person underestimates how a different emotional or bodily state changes preferences and behavior.

**Positive:** While comfortably full, I insist being hungry would never alter my patience, despite repeatedly becoming irritable when hungry.

**Target negative:** I schedule a break because I know hunger may affect my patience.

**Boundary:** Require a mismatch across states or failure to understand another person's state.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/empathy-gap)

## 36. Endowment Effect

`endowment_effect` · cognitive_concept

Ownership raises valuation beyond the value assigned before owning the same item.

**Positive:** I would pay €20 for this identical mug, but once it became mine I demanded €60 solely because it was mine.

**Target negative:** I ask €60 because this mug has documented features absent from the €20 version.

**Boundary:** Ownership must explain the valuation gap; market differences and transaction costs may explain it instead.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/endowment-effect)

## 37. Extrinsic Incentive Bias

`extrinsic_incentive_bias` · cognitive_concept

Others' motivation is attributed more to external rewards than one's own motivation is.

**Positive:** I work for the intellectual challenge, but my equally enthusiastic colleagues must only care about bonuses; I have no evidence of that difference.

**Target negative:** My colleague told me she accepted this assignment specifically for its bonus.

**Boundary:** Require an unsupported self-other asymmetry, not any mention of money.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/extrinsic-incentive-bias)

## 38. False Consensus Effect

`false_consensus_effect` · cognitive_concept

Agreement with one's own position is overestimated.

**Positive:** I dislike open offices, so I estimate almost everyone dislikes them, although the representative survey shows an even split.

**Target negative:** I dislike open offices but know the survey shows divided preferences.

**Boundary:** Different from pluralistic ignorance, which involves a mistaken belief that one's private view is not shared.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/false-consensus-effect)

## 39. Framing Effect

`framing_effect` · cognitive_concept

Equivalent information yields different choices when expressed differently.

**Positive:** I reject the service labeled '10% unsuccessful' but accept the identical service labeled '90% successful.'

**Target negative:** I choose the service with a verified 95% success rate over one with 90%.

**Boundary:** Check mathematical and substantive equivalence; genuinely different information is not framing alone.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/framing-effect)

## 40. Functional Fixedness

`functional_fixedness` · cognitive_concept

An object's customary purpose blocks recognition of another useful purpose.

**Positive:** We need a temporary weight, but I overlook the heavy book because I think books can only be used for reading.

**Target negative:** I avoid using the book as a weight because it would damage a fragile surface.

**Boundary:** Object-use restriction differs from fixation on an established problem-solving procedure.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/functional-fixedness)

## 41. Fundamental Attribution Error

`fundamental_attribution_error` · cognitive_concept

Behavior is attributed too strongly to character while relevant situational constraints are discounted.

**Positive:** Her call dropped during a documented network outage; that proves she is inconsiderate, whatever the outage did.

**Target negative:** The outage explains the dropped call; it tells me little about her character.

**Boundary:** A character judgment needs evidence of neglected context to support this label confidently.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/fundamental-attribution-error)

## 42. Gambler's Fallacy

`gamblers_fallacy` · cognitive_concept

Independent random outcomes are expected to reverse because of previous outcomes.

**Positive:** This fair coin produced five heads, so tails is now more likely on the next independent toss.

**Target negative:** The next toss still has a 50% chance of tails.

**Boundary:** Require independence or stable probabilities; sampling without replacement can change the odds.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/gamblers-fallacy)

## 43. Google Effect

`google_effect` · cognitive_concept

Expected access to external information reduces memory for the information itself.

**Positive:** When told I could reopen the saved lookup, I remembered where it was but retained less of its content than when told it would disappear.

**Target negative:** I used a search engine and then accurately recalled the information.

**Boundary:** External lookup alone is insufficient; this is a memory/offloading effect, not automatically faulty reasoning.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/google-effect)

## 44. Halo Effect

`halo_effect` · cognitive_concept

A favorable impression on one dimension spills into an unsupported judgment on another.

**Positive:** The applicant is stylish, so she must also be excellent at debugging; I have not assessed that skill.

**Target negative:** The applicant solved our debugging exercise accurately, so I rated that skill highly.

**Boundary:** Require cross-attribute spillover; competence inferred from relevant performance is different.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/halo-effect)

## 45. Hard-easy effect

`hard_easy_effect` · cognitive_concept

Confidence tends to be too high for difficult tasks and too low for easy tasks.

**Positive:** Across a scored test, my difficult-item confidence averaged 80% with 30% accuracy, while easy-item confidence averaged 70% with 95% accuracy.

**Target negative:** My confidence approximately matched my accuracy in both difficulty groups.

**Boundary:** This is a calibration pattern across difficulty levels, not simply being overconfident once.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/hard-easy-effect)

## 46. Heuristics

`heuristics` · cognitive_concept

A simplified rule reduces the information or computation used to decide.

**Positive:** When short of time, I use a rough rule to select a route rather than calculating every alternative.

**Target negative:** I exhaustively evaluated all routes using the specified objective.

**Boundary:** Umbrella concept; prefer a named subtype when supported, and do not assume a shortcut is inaccurate.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/heuristics)

## 47. Hindsight Bias

`hindsight_bias` · cognitive_concept

Knowing the outcome makes it seem more predictable beforehand than it was.

**Positive:** I said the match was impossible to call. After the result, I insist I knew the winner all along.

**Target negative:** My dated pre-match prediction named the winner and expressed the same confidence I report now.

**Boundary:** Different from judging the quality of the original decision by its outcome.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/hindsight-bias)

## 48. Hot Hand Fallacy

`hot_hand_fallacy` · cognitive_concept

A success streak is treated as evidence of increased next-trial success without support.

**Positive:** The independent random generator gave me three wins, so I am on a streak and my next win is more likely.

**Target negative:** The generator's independent win probability remains unchanged.

**Boundary:** Real persistence can exist in skill-based activities; do not label every streak inference a fallacy.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/hot-hand-fallacy)

## 49. Hyperbolic Discounting

`hyperbolic_discounting` · cognitive_concept

Impatience decreases with delay, producing preferences consistent with non-exponential discounting.

**Positive:** I choose €40 today over €50 tomorrow, but choose €50 in 31 days over €40 in 30 days under otherwise identical conditions.

**Target negative:** I use the same proportional discount rate for equivalent intervals at all delays.

**Boundary:** This reversal supports decreasing impatience but does not prove an exact hyperbolic function; it overlaps present bias.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/hyperbolic-discounting)

## 50. IKEA Effect

`ikea_effect` · cognitive_concept

Personal assembly or creation inflates an object's perceived value.

**Positive:** I rank my self-assembled shelf above an indistinguishable ready-made one solely because I built it.

**Target negative:** My shelf is more valuable to me because its custom dimensions fit my room.

**Boundary:** Separate construction effort from ownership alone and from objective customization benefits.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/ikea-effect)

## 51. Identifiable Victim Effect

`identifiable_victim_effect` · cognitive_concept

An identified individual elicits more help than comparable anonymous beneficiaries.

**Positive:** I donate more when shown one named beneficiary than when the identical need is presented anonymously, with effectiveness held constant.

**Target negative:** I choose the program with verified greater benefit per donation.

**Boundary:** Identification must drive the difference; differing effectiveness can justify allocation.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/identifiable-victim-effect)

## 52. Illusion of Control

`illusion_of_control` · cognitive_concept

Personal actions are believed to control outcomes they cannot influence.

**Positive:** Pressing the lottery button with my lucky finger makes this independent random draw more likely to win.

**Target negative:** Pressing the button starts the draw but does not change its odds.

**Boundary:** Actual causal influence is not illusory control.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/illusion-of-control)

## 53. Illusion of Transparency

`illusion_of_transparency` · cognitive_concept

Internal feelings or intentions are assumed to be more visible to others than they are.

**Positive:** Although observers could not tell I was nervous, I was certain they could read every anxious thought from my neutral expression.

**Target negative:** I told the audience I was nervous, so they knew.

**Boundary:** Internal-state visibility differs from believing one receives unusually much attention.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/illusion-of-transparency)

## 54. Illusion of Validity

`illusion_of_validity` · cognitive_concept

A coherent impression creates unwarranted confidence in the validity of a prediction.

**Positive:** These unvalidated interview impressions form such a consistent story that I am certain they predict performance, despite near-chance results in past tests.

**Target negative:** I report uncertainty because the interview method lacks predictive validation.

**Boundary:** Focus on confidence in an unreliable inference, not confidence alone.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/illusion-of-validity)

## 55. Illusory Correlation

`illusory_correlation` · cognitive_concept

A relationship is perceived despite insufficient evidence or contrary association data.

**Positive:** I believe rainy days cause software failures because the pair stands out in memory, although the logs show equal failure rates on wet and dry days.

**Target negative:** The logs show an association; further work is needed to assess causation.

**Boundary:** Distinguish an unsupported association from the separate mistake of treating a real correlation as causation.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/illusory-correlation)

## 56. Illusory Truth Effect

`illusory_truth_effect` · cognitive_concept

Repeated exposure makes a claim seem truer without new supporting evidence.

**Positive:** The rumor sounded doubtful initially, but after seeing the same unsourced sentence twenty times I now think it is true.

**Target negative:** I believed the claim after independent records verified it.

**Boundary:** Repeated independent evidence differs from repetition of the same unsupported claim.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/illusory-truth-effect)

## 57. Impact Bias

`impact_bias` · cognitive_concept

The intensity or duration of future emotional reactions is overestimated.

**Positive:** I predicted losing the contest would make every day miserable for a year; a month later I had largely adjusted.

**Target negative:** I expect disappointment but do not know how intense or lasting it will be.

**Boundary:** An emotional forecast needs a basis for judging overestimation; intense feelings alone are not bias.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/impact-bias)

## 58. In-group Bias

`in_group_bias` · cognitive_concept

Shared group membership leads to preferential evaluation or treatment.

**Positive:** The candidates performed equally, but I rated the one from my own club higher solely because she is one of us.

**Target negative:** I selected the candidate with stronger relevant performance regardless of club.

**Boundary:** Group membership must drive unequal treatment; relevant qualifications are an alternative explanation.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/in-group-bias)

## 59. Incentivization

`incentivization` · cognitive_concept

Rewards or penalties alter motivation or behavior.

**Positive:** When each completed practice set earned a token, participants completed more sets than in the otherwise identical no-token condition.

**Target negative:** Practice increased before any reward was introduced, with no change after it.

**Boundary:** A motivational mechanism, not inherently a cognitive bias or evidence of manipulation.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/incentivization)

## 60. Just-world Hypothesis

`just_world_hypothesis` · cognitive_concept

People's outcomes are assumed to reflect what they morally deserve.

**Positive:** She was cheated, so she must have done something to deserve it; innocent people would not suffer that outcome.

**Target negative:** The evidence shows she was deceived; suffering does not establish wrongdoing.

**Boundary:** Do not infer this label from a supported claim about consequences of a specific action.

[Concept source](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/just-world-hypothesis)

## 61. Lag Effect

`lag_effect` · cognitive_concept

Within spaced practice, longer suitable intervals can improve later retention relative to shorter intervals.

**Positive:** With equal study time and a fixed later test, the group with longer review gaps remembered more than the short-gap group.

**Target negative:** The groups had identical gaps but different total study time.

**Positive:** Başkan değişti, işsizlik düştü; demek ki düşüşü başkan sağladı.

## 122. Bağlam eksiltme

`contextual_omission` · media_technique

Erişilebilir ve ilgili bağlamın dışarıda bırakılması karşılaştırmayı değiştirir.

**Positive:** Gelir arttı deniyor; aynı dönemde fiyatların daha hızlı arttığı verisi atlanıyor.

**Target negative:** Gelir nominal arttı, fiyatlara göre azaldı.

**Boundary:** Omission Bias ile aynı değildir; tam metin ve ilgili karşı kanıt olmadan adaydır.
